HETU Governance Mechanism Analysis

HETU's governance vision is to build an autonomous ecosystem that integrates stability, adaptability, and decentralization. Through role division and checks and balances, it ensures the network can resist malicious attacks and centralization risks while maintaining vitality and continuous evolution.

I. Overall Governance Model Architecture

HETU's governance architecture can be seen as a "separation of powers" three-dimensional model, where each role has final say in specific areas but is constrained by other roles.

  • Hetu Pod (Core Guardian): Responsible for the final technical implementation and security of the core protocol, representing the protocol's long-termism and stability.
  • Intersubjective Foundation (Legal and Emergency Affairs Guardian): As a legal entity in the real world and a "safety valve" for governance, responsible for legal compliance and rapid response to emergencies.
  • DAO Committee (Ecosystem Strategy and Resource Allocator): Represents the collective wisdom of the community, responsible for ecosystem development direction, budget allocation, and supervision and incubation of subnets.
  • HETU Holder Community (Embodiment of Ultimate Sovereignty): All HETU token stakers are the foundation of decentralized power, expressing collective will through on-chain voting.

II. Roles, Responsibilities, and Checks and Balances Mechanisms at Each Level

1. Hetu Pod

  • Core Authority:
    • Technical Veto Power (Veto Power): Has the final review and veto power over major changes to the core protocol that have passed community voting.
    • Protocol Framework Maintenance: Responsible for daily maintenance, version releases, and security patches of the protocol codebase.
  • Incentive Source: Receives approximately 3% annual network inflation tokens as incentives for continuous development.
  • Checks and Balances Mechanisms:
    1. No Authority to Initiate Changes: Can only passively review proposals passed by the community.
    2. Constraint by Social Consensus: The community can choose to run a new version through a hard fork, causing a Pod that abuses power to lose influence.
    3. Reputation and Incentives: Malicious behavior leads to loss of reputation and cessation of incentives.

2. Intersubjective Foundation

  • Core Authority:
    • Emergency Governance: Has multi-signature controlled emergency intervention rights when facing serious security vulnerabilities.
    • Legal Responsibility: As a legal entity, signs cooperation agreements, manages intellectual property, etc.
    • Ecosystem Cold Start: Helps ecosystem early development by running nodes, delegating stakes to subnets, etc.
  • Checks and Balances Mechanisms:
    1. Limited and Transparent Power: Emergency powers require post-facto reporting and ratification by the community.
    2. Power Diminishes as Ecosystem Matures: Its role in the network will gradually be replaced by the community and DAO.
    3. No Access to Core Protocol: Has no authority to modify core protocol code.

3. DAO Federation and Core Committee

  • Core Authority:
    • Proposal Screening and Guidance: Improves the efficiency and quality of on-chain voting (HIP).
    • Treasury Fund Allocation: Manages and allocates DAO treasury funds to support ecosystem projects.
    • Subnet Supervision and Incubation: Responsible for reviewing and launching new subnets.
  • Checks and Balances Mechanisms:
    1. Election and Removal Mechanism: Committee members are elected by the community and can be removed.
    2. Community Voting Veto: Major fund usage requires approval through HETU holder voting.
    3. Decentralization through Professional DAOs: The DAO Federation model decentralizes power into different professional areas.

4. HETU Holder Community

  • Core Authority:
    • Proposal and Voting (HIP): Initiates and decides all important decisions.
    • Election and Removal: Elects DAO core committee members.
    • Ultimate Sovereignty: Holds the theoretical highest power to modify the governance rules themselves.
  • Checks and Balances Mechanisms ("Preventing Tyranny of the Majority"):
    1. Pod's Technical Veto Power: Prevents technically harmful proposals.
    2. Reputation Score Mechanism: Reduces weight determined solely by token holdings.
    3. Governance Layering: Professionalizes decision-making authority to improve decision quality.
    4. Delegation Mechanism: Ordinary token holders can delegate voting rights to experts.

IV. Key Ecosystem Role Positioning

  • Advaita Labs: Core research and development and intensive incubation lab, similar to Consensys for Ethereum, receiving funding for development and achieving self-sustainability through incubating subnets.
  • ChaosChain (AI Governance Subnet): An innovative governance subnet that uses delegated HETU rewards to run AI models, assisting off-chain governance, such as analyzing proposal impacts and detecting public sentiment.
  • Flux (Decentralized Exchange): Core financial infrastructure within the ecosystem, used for asset exchange and governance incentive distribution.

From a crypto-native perspective, the core of checks and balances among roles lies in:

  1. Separation and Verification of Powers: No single entity has absolute power.
  2. Economic Incentive Alignment: All participants' benefits are tied to the long-term health of the ecosystem.
  3. Transparency and Exitability: Decisions are public, and the ultimate check is a hard fork driven by social consensus.
